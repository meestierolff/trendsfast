A post can be recent, popular, and relevant without showing a trend. Those are different claims, and collapsing them creates bad distribution decisions.

## Start with the claim you can actually support

“Published two hours ago” is a freshness claim. “Has 500 likes” is a point-in-time engagement claim. Neither tells you whether attention is rising, whether that level is unusual for the source, or whether independent people are converging on the same problem.

Momentum needs a direction. The cleanest evidence is a provider-supplied time series or two comparable, time-separated observations. Without that, the honest claim may be narrower: an emerging conversation is unusually relevant right now.

## Popularity is local to a source

Five hundred reactions can be exceptional for one account and ordinary for another. Raw engagement also means different things across search, GitHub, Hacker News, X, and YouTube. A useful system compares like with like and records what was actually observed.

That is why TrendsFast separates measured external series, internally observed velocity, cross-source corroboration, emerging signals, and insufficient evidence. The label constrains the language an agent is allowed to use.

## Copies do not become confirmation

Ten articles can all descend from one product announcement. Counting domains would make that look like broad agreement, even though the information has one origin. Independent confirmation requires lineage: different sources arriving at the same opportunity through genuinely separate observations.

This matters because distribution copy often jumps from “people are discussing this” to “the market is shifting.” Evidence lineage keeps the second sentence from outrunning the first.

## Relevance still comes first

Even measured momentum may be useless to a specific founder. The audience may be wrong, the founder may have no credible angle, the format may be unrealistic, or the channel may be saturated.

A trend tool for founders should therefore answer two questions in order:

1. What does the evidence support?
2. What can this product credibly do with it now?

The output is not a universal trend score. It is one bounded distribution decision with a truth class, original receipts, limitations, confidence, and a validity window.

Recent is useful context. Trending is an evidence claim. Relevant is a product decision. Keeping those separate is what makes the final move trustworthy.
